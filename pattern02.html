Pattern 02: Recommendation Trap

Intent

Identify situations where an AI system's practical influence over a decision increases without a corresponding change in its formally delegated authority.

The pattern is particularly concerned with the transition from Recommend to Constrain that can occur through changes in human behaviour and workflow rather than through an explicit authority decision.

Problem

An AI system is introduced to provide recommendations while a human retains formal decision authority.

As confidence in the system grows, people naturally adapt how they work with it. Recommendations become the starting point for human judgement rather than one input among several.

Over time, the human may shift from:

considering a recommendation and making a decision

to:

confirming a recommendation unless there is a reason to challenge it.

No formal authority has been delegated to the system. Yet its influence over the decision has increased.

The resulting governance problem is that formal authority no longer provides a reliable indication of practical influence.

Forces / Trade-offs

The pattern is driven by several mutually reinforcing forces:

  • Efficiency: accepting a reliable recommendation is faster than independently reviewing all available information.
  • Reliability: successful recommendations increase confidence in the system.
  • Consistency: following the recommendation produces a more standardised workflow.
  • Cognitive effort: challenging a recommendation requires more effort than accepting it.
  • Accountability: the human remains formally responsible for the decision, even as the system increasingly shapes it.
  • Automation pressure: once the recommendation is routinely accepted, further automation becomes an apparently natural next step.

The central tension is between human deliberation and operational efficiency.

Applicability

Applies where:

  • AI recommendations inform consequential human decisions.
  • Humans retain formal decision authority.
  • Recommendations can influence what receives attention or scrutiny.
  • The cost of independently evaluating each case creates pressure to rely on the system.

Examples include:

  • lending and credit assessment
  • clinical decision support
  • insurance underwriting
  • recruitment
  • fraud detection
  • compliance review
  • security operations
  • professional judgement workflows

Authority Stage(s)

Primarily:

Recommend → Constrain

The system is initially operating at Recommend: it proposes or highlights a course of action while the human remains the decision-maker.

The pattern emerges when the recommendation begins to shape the available decision space: what receives attention, what is investigated, and what appears to be the default outcome.

The system can therefore begin exhibiting characteristics of Constrain without an explicit transition in formal authority.

Actors

  • AI system: generates recommendations, classifications, rankings or proposed actions.
  • Human decision-maker: retains formal decision authority.
  • Workflow owner: designs or modifies the process in which recommendations are consumed.
  • Governance function: defines and reviews the intended authority boundary.
  • Organisation: remains accountable for the resulting decisions and outcomes.

Interaction

The key interaction is not simply:

AI → Human → Decision

but the gradual transformation of that interaction:

Initial:

AI recommendation
       ↓
Human considers evidence
       ↓
Independent judgement
       ↓
Decision

into:

Mature workflow:

AI recommendation
       ↓
Human starts from recommendation
       ↓
Challenge if necessary
       ↓
Decision

The formal decision point remains human.

The practical starting point has moved to the AI.

Decision Flow

A typical progression is:

Recommendation introduced
        ↓
Recommendation proves useful
        ↓
Human confidence increases
        ↓
Recommendation becomes starting point
        ↓
Attention is allocated according to recommendation
        ↓
Alternative judgements require additional effort
        ↓
Human increasingly confirms rather than evaluates
        ↓
System begins to constrain the decision space

The critical governance point is the transition between evaluation and confirmation.

Governance Implications

Governance should not assess authority solely by asking:

"Who has the final decision?"

It should also examine:

  • What does the human see first?
  • What receives human attention?
  • Which cases receive additional scrutiny?
  • How often are recommendations challenged?
  • What effort is required to override the recommendation?
  • Has the workflow been redesigned around the AI's output?
  • Has the human's role changed from judgement to confirmation?

The pattern suggests that formal authority is a lagging indicator of practical influence.

A governance review that observes only explicit permissions may therefore miss a transition from Recommend toward Constrain.

Failure Modes

  • Confirmation behaviour: humans routinely approve recommendations without independent evaluation.
  • Attention bias: recommendations determine which cases receive detailed scrutiny.
  • Override friction: challenging the system becomes materially harder than accepting it.
  • Workflow capture: surrounding processes become structured around the system's recommendations.
  • Authority ambiguity: humans remain formally accountable while practical influence shifts toward the system.
  • Silent progression: the system crosses into constraining behaviour without an explicit authority review.

Related Patterns

  • Context Creep: expanded context can increase the significance of recommendations without changing formal authority.
