Patterns
  • Pattern 01 - Context Creep

One of the more subtle ways AI systems become consequential has nothing to do with better models or new capabilities.

It happens through Context Creep.

Context Creep is when an AI system's accessible context expands gradually through a series of individually reasonable integrations, while nobody stops to reassess what that means if the system is later trusted with more responsibility.

No single integration feels like a governance decision. That's precisely why it's easy to miss.

Take a clinical triage assistant.

It starts with a narrow role: reading symptoms captured during patient intake and suggesting a priority level for a clinician to review. Every recommendation is checked before anything happens.

The system proves useful, so its context grows. It gains access to medical history. Then medication records. Pathology results. Previous admissions. Later, clinician notes, allergies and data from wearable devices.

Each addition makes sense. Better context leads to better recommendations.

On paper, very little has changed. The assistant still isn't diagnosing patients or making decisions independently.

Months later, the organisation proposes a seemingly modest change: allow the assistant to route low-risk patients directly into standard treatment pathways.

At first glance, the new authority appears limited.

But this isn't the system that was originally assessed.

Its decisions are now informed by a far richer view of each patient. Misread a pathology trend, overlook a drug interaction, or place too much weight on an historical diagnosis, and a patient may be directed down the wrong pathway before a clinician ever reviews the case.

The request for authority is new. The exposure isn't.

That changed quietly over months, one integration at a time.

The governance discussion focused on the decision being requested, because that was the visible change. Exposure had been changing all along.

That's Context Creep.

Pattern: Context Creep

Observable signal: "We're only connecting one more data source."

Underlying shift: Context expands quietly while the consequences of future decisions grow with it.

Governance blind spot: Authority changes usually require review. Context changes rarely do.

SCOPE principle: Governance should reassess exposure whenever context expands—not only when authority does.
